1. Product Purpose
The purpose of the Shunting Safety Software is to provide a simple central application for managing, monitoring and auditing shunting safety devices installed and used within Railway yards.
The application will allow Railway users to:
Create and configure a Railway yard.
Configure the pit lines, stabling lines, sidings and other lines available in the yard.
Maintain exactly one dead end for every configured line.
Register fixed and portable shunting devices within the yard.
Assign each fixed Dead-End Device to a specific line.
Issue portable devices to a Pointsman, Shunter or authorised Railway employee.
Automatically receive device data when devices are powered on.
Automatically identify when a Loco Device connects with a Dead-End Device.
Automatically create and close a shunting session.
View ongoing shunting activity live.
Monitor the working condition of all fixed Dead-End Devices.
Maintain SIM, installation, maintenance and battery information.
Maintain permanent audit logs.
Generate daily and historical reports in Excel and PDF.
Product BoundaryThe application is not a complete yard-operation planning or train-management system. Its primary purpose is device management, live safety monitoring, health monitoring, operational audit and reporting.

2. Business Problem
Railway yards may contain several pit lines, stabling lines and sidings. Fixed Dead-End Devices are installed at the dead ends of these lines. Portable Loco Devices are used during shunting operations and can communicate with different Dead-End Devices within the same yard.
Without a central software platform, Railway users may not have a reliable digital record of:
Which devices are installed in a yard.
Which line a Dead-End Device is installed on.
Whether a Dead-End Device is operational.
When a device last communicated with the cloud.
Which portable device was issued to which employee.
Which Loco Device connected with which Dead-End Device.
When a shunting session started and ended.
What the final placement distance was from the dead end.
Whether any device communication failed.
Whether SIM recharge or maintenance is due.
How many operations occurred during a day.
The proposed software will create one central and auditable source of truth for all these activities.

4. Assumptions
4.1 Organisation Assumptions
Railway Zone and Division values will be predefined or hard-coded.
Separate Zone and Division configuration screens are not required in the MVP.
Station will be selected or entered while creating the yard.
Multiple yards can be created in the application.
A yard belongs to one station, one division and one zone.
4.2 Yard and Line Assumptions
A yard may contain multiple lines.
Supported line types include Pit Line, Stabling Line, Siding, Inspection Line, Maintenance Line and Other.
Every configured line has exactly one dead end.
A line cannot have multiple dead ends.
Dead-end information will be captured directly within the line configuration.
A separate Dead-End Location Configuration screen is not required.
A line may have one active Dead-End Device installed at its dead end.
A line may temporarily have no active Dead-End Device when the device is under maintenance, removed or awaiting replacement.
Replacement history must be retained.

Dead-End Device and Loco Device are active in the initial deployment.
Pointsman Device and Coupling Device may remain configured but inactive until required.
The Loco Device includes its TFT/HMI. The TFT/HMI will not be registered separately.
4.4 Communication Assumptions
Dead-End Devices and Loco Devices have GSM/SIM connectivity.
Devices also communicate locally through RF.
Local device-to-device communication does not depend on the cloud.
Device data is automatically sent to the cloud when GSM is available.
When GSM is unavailable, devices may store events locally and upload them later, subject to hardware capability.
The original device event time must be preserved.
The cloud receives device heartbeat, connection, disconnection, distance, warning/fault and acknowledgement events.
4.5 Session Assumptions
No user manually creates a shunting session.
One LD-to-DE connection creates one shunting session.
A session starts when a valid connection-start event is received.
A session ends when a disconnection event is received or when no communication is received for a configurable inactivity period.
One Loco Device may connect with different Dead-End Devices during the same shift.
Each LD-to-DE connection is recorded as a separate session.
Sessions may be grouped in reports by employee, issue record, shift, date and yard.
LD and DE must belong to the same yard for a valid session.
Cross-yard communication attempts are rejected or flagged.
4.6 Heartbeat Assumptions
Fixed Dead-End Devices are powered devices and are not primarily battery-operated.
Each Dead-End Device sends a heartbeat every 30 minutes.
Heartbeat frequency is configurable in the backend.
The application monitors missed heartbeats and calculates health automatically.
Device health alerts are displayed in the application.
SMS and email notifications may be introduced later.
4.7 Portable Device Issue Assumptions
LD, PD and CD devices belong to the portable device pool of a yard.
A portable device may be issued to an authorised employee.
The software records who took the device, issue time, return time and condition at issue/return.
A portable device cannot be issued to two employees simultaneously.
Sessions created during the issue period are linked to the issue record.

15. Automatic Shunting Session Logic
15.1 Session Start
A shunting session starts automatically when all of the following conditions are met:
An LD connects with a DE.
Both UUIDs are registered.
Both devices are Active.
Both devices belong to the same yard.
The DE is assigned to an active line.
A valid connection-start event is received.

15.3 Session End
A session ends when a device-disconnection event is received or when no communication is received for the configured inactivity timeout. The suggested initial timeout is 5 minutes, configurable between 5 and 10 minutes.
15.4 Final Placement Distance Logic
DefinitionFinal Placement Distance is the last valid and stable distance received from the Dead-End Device before the session ends. Minimum Distance Recorded and Final Placement Distance must be stored separately.

A distance is considered stable when similar readings are received over a configurable number of samples or when the device reports that movement has stopped.

26. Business Rules
Zone and Division are predefined values.
A yard must be created before lines or devices are registered.
One yard can contain multiple lines.
Every line has exactly one dead end.
Dead-end details are stored within the line.
One line can have only one active DE device.
Every device must belong to a yard.
A DE must be assigned to a line.
Portable devices are not permanently assigned to a line.
Portable devices are issued to an employee.
A device cannot be issued to two employees simultaneously.
An LD-DE connection automatically creates a session.
No manual operation creation is required.
One session contains one LD and one DE.
An LD connecting to another DE creates another session.
Sessions are linked to the active issue record.
Session closure is automatic after disconnection or timeout.
DE heartbeat is expected every 30 minutes.
Missing heartbeats create health alerts.
Device and session history cannot be deleted.
Excel and PDF reports must be available.
DE devices are powered devices.
LD TFT/HMI is part of the LD and not separately registered.
Initial deployment may use only LD and DE.
PD and CD remain available for future activation.

30. Final Product Summary
The Shunting Safety Software will be a simple yard-centric platform. The permanent configuration is:
Yard  ↓Multiple Lines  ↓Exactly One Dead End Per Line  ↓One Active Dead-End Device

Portable devices belong to the yard device pool and are issued to authorised staff. An actual shunting activity is identified automatically when:
Loco Device Connects with Dead-End Device        ↓Session Starts Automatically        ↓Live Data Is Shown        ↓Distance and Alerts Are Recorded        ↓Devices Disconnect        ↓Session Ends Automatically        ↓Audit and Reports Are Generated

The application must provide clear answers to the following questions:
Which yards are configured?
What lines exist in each yard?
Which Dead-End Device is installed on each line?
Which portable devices belong to the yard?
Who took a portable device?
When was it issued and returned?
Which LD connected with which DE?
When did the session start and end?
What was the final placement distance?
Were there communication or device failures?
When was the last heartbeat received?
Which devices are healthy or offline?
Which SIMs and maintenance activities are due?
Can the complete daily record be downloaded in Excel or PDF?
MVP OutcomeA focused application for yard configuration, device registration, automatic shunting-session monitoring, Dead-End Device health verification, audit history and downloadable reports.
